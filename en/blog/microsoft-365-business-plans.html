Understanding the Microsoft 365 Business range

Compare Microsoft 365 Business Basic, Standard and Premium, then see how Defender, Purview and data governance can prepare your organisation for Copilot.

Microsoft 365By Aslam Gangat28 October 2026

Understanding the Microsoft 365 Business range

Choosing a Microsoft 365 licence is not only about deciding whether Word, Excel or Outlook should be installed on a computer. Device ownership, remote work, access rights, data sensitivity and the required level of security are equally important.

This article explains the differences between Microsoft 365 Business Basic, Business Standard and Business Premium. It also considers how different licences can be combined within the same organisation before examining the additional protection available through the Microsoft Defender and Microsoft Purview suites.

Finally, we will address an increasingly important requirement: preparing identities, permissions and business data before deploying Microsoft 365 Copilot.

By the end of the article, you should be able to understand your current subscription more clearly, recognise its main limitations and identify the appropriate level of productivity, management and security for each user profile.

Business Basic: working and collaborating in the cloud

Microsoft 365 Business Basic is designed for users who work mainly with web and mobile applications.

It provides access to business email, file storage and sharing, as well as communication and collaboration services. It does not, however, include the desktop versions of the Microsoft 365 applications installed on a computer.

Business Basic can therefore suit an employee who works primarily through a browser and does not regularly need installed versions of Word, Excel, PowerPoint or Outlook.

Its limitations become more significant when users need to work offline, use advanced desktop application features or access business information from a device that the organisation wants to manage and secure centrally.

Business Standard: adding installed desktop applications

Microsoft 365 Business Standard includes the collaboration services available with Business Basic and adds the desktop versions of applications such as Word, Excel, PowerPoint and Outlook.

It is suitable for employees who regularly create complex documents, rely on advanced Microsoft 365 application features or need to continue working when their internet connection is limited.

One distinction is particularly important: moving from Business Basic to Business Standard is primarily a productivity upgrade.

Business Standard does not provide the same foundation for identity management, device management and threat protection as Business Premium. A computer with Microsoft 365 desktop applications installed is not necessarily a centrally managed and secured device.

When a change of plan also requires the transfer of mailboxes, files, accounts or Microsoft 365 environments, the MC3 Cloud migration services can help define the scope and support the transition to the target environment.

Business Premium: managing and securing the workplace

Microsoft 365 Business Premium combines productivity applications and services with additional management and security capabilities.

For many small and medium-sized businesses using company-owned computers and mobile devices, Business Premium provides a coherent foundation for managing identities, access, endpoints, email and selected categories of sensitive information.

Controlling identities and access with Microsoft Entra ID Plan 1

Microsoft Entra ID Plan 1 enables the organisation to define access policies for business resources.

Conditional Access can take the context of a sign-in into account before granting access to an application or business data.

Relevant risks include:

  • use of a stolen password;
  • sign-in from a device that does not meet company requirements;
  • access to sensitive resources under insufficiently secure conditions;
  • lack of controls that reflect the sensitivity of the resource.

The MC3 Cloud identity and endpoint management service can help structure access policies, account management and device administration.

Managing devices with Microsoft Intune Plan 1

Microsoft Intune Plan 1 supports the management of computers and mobile devices that access business resources.

The organisation can define configuration and compliance policies, manage selected business applications and apply more consistent controls to authorised devices.

Relevant risks include:

  • an inadequately configured or outdated computer;
  • inconsistent security settings across devices;
  • loss or theft of a device containing business information;
  • access remaining active after an employee leaves;
  • uncontrolled mixing of personal and business data on a mobile device.

The identity and endpoint management service can support policy design, device onboarding and the establishment of a consistent management foundation.

Protecting endpoints with Microsoft Defender for Business

Microsoft Defender for Business protects computers, tablets and phones against threats targeting endpoints. It is included with Microsoft 365 Business Premium.

Relevant risks include:

  • malware installation;
  • ransomware attempts;
  • suspicious behaviour on a computer;
  • exploitation of a vulnerable device;
  • malicious activity that does not resemble a traditional antivirus infection.

Endpoint protection is therefore not limited to scanning files. It also helps the organisation identify abnormal activity on business devices.

Strengthening email protection

Microsoft 365 Business Premium includes Microsoft Defender for Office 365 Plan 1 to strengthen protection for email and collaboration services.

Relevant risks include:

  • a phishing message designed to steal a password;
  • a link leading to a fraudulent website;
  • a malicious attachment;
  • a dangerous file shared through a collaboration service;
  • an impersonation attempt intended to trigger a payment or disclosure of sensitive information.

Introducing information protection with Microsoft Purview

Business Premium also includes Microsoft Purview information protection capabilities, including sensitivity labelling and data loss prevention features for email and files.

Relevant risks include:

  • accidental transmission of a confidential document;
  • excessive sharing of a sensitive file;
  • personal information being sent to an unauthorised recipient;
  • failure to identify documents requiring additional protection.

Business Premium provides these capabilities, but their effectiveness depends on configuration. Purchasing a licence does not automatically deploy a security policy tailored to the organisation’s risks.

A Microsoft 365 security assessment can review the configuration, identify gaps across identities, devices, email and data, and establish a prioritised action plan.

Business Premium and Copilot: govern data before introducing AI

Microsoft 365 Copilot can find, summarise and use business information from data that the user is already authorised to access.

This makes data governance particularly important. If an employee has access to a SharePoint site, document or workspace with overly broad permissions, Copilot may use that content when responding on behalf of the user.

Copilot does not necessarily create a new access right. It can, however, make information that was already accessible much easier to find and use, even when the organisation is not fully aware that the permission exists.

Before deploying Copilot, the organisation should therefore review:

  • who can access SharePoint sites, Teams workspaces and OneDrive files;
  • whether documents are shared more broadly than necessary;
  • whether historical groups and permissions are still justified;
  • which information should be classified as sensitive;
  • which sharing rules should be strengthened;
  • which data should be retained, protected or deleted;
  • how Copilot activity will be monitored and governed.

Microsoft recommends establishing a secure and governed data foundation for Copilot by addressing oversharing, implementing guardrails and meeting applicable regulatory requirements.

Business Premium provides an initial foundation through Microsoft Entra ID, Microsoft Intune and selected Microsoft Purview capabilities. Depending on the sensitivity of the information and the organisation’s requirements, additional Purview capabilities may be necessary.

The MC3 Cloud Microsoft Purview data protection and compliance service can help assess classification, sensitivity labels, data loss prevention, retention and the controls required before or during Copilot adoption.

Can different plans be combined within the same organisation?

An organisation does not have to assign the same licence to every employee. Different Microsoft 365 plans can be combined according to user needs, devices, access rights and the sensitivity of the data involved.

A possible approach is:

  • Business Basic for users who work mainly with web applications;
  • Business Standard for users who need installed Microsoft 365 applications;
  • Business Premium for employees whose identities and devices must be centrally managed and secured;
  • Business Premium with the Defender and Purview suites for highly exposed users or people handling particularly sensitive information.

Licences should not be assigned solely according to job title. A mobile salesperson, HR manager, accountant or IT administrator may require advanced protection because of their access rights or the information they handle.

A mixed-licence approach can help control costs. Excessive segmentation can also make administration more complex, create protection gaps and make security policies harder to apply.

Four questions should be considered before assigning a licence:

  1. Which applications does the user need?
  2. Which devices and working environments will the user rely on?
  3. Which resources and data can the user access?
  4. What would be the impact if the user’s account or device were compromised?

A service:microsoft-cost-license-optimization can compare assigned licences with actual requirements, identify underused capabilities and highlight potential overlap with third-party products.

Defender Suite: detecting and responding to advanced attacks

The Microsoft Defender Suite for Microsoft 365 Business Premium extends the security capabilities already included with Business Premium.

It provides additional protection across identities, endpoints, email, collaboration tools and cloud applications. Business Premium is a prerequisite for this add-on.

Microsoft Entra ID Plan 2: identifying risky identities

Microsoft Entra ID Plan 2 adds advanced identity protection and governance capabilities.

Relevant risks include:

  • sign-ins displaying risk signals;
  • use of compromised credentials;
  • unnecessary retention of sensitive access rights;
  • inadequate management of privileged accounts;
  • failure to review previously granted access.

Microsoft Defender for Identity: monitoring identity-based attacks

Microsoft Defender for Identity provides threat detection and response capabilities for identities.

Relevant risks include:

  • an attacker moving through the environment after compromising an initial account;
  • attempts to reach an identity with higher privileges;
  • suspicious activity targeting accounts and identity infrastructure;
  • abnormal use of business credentials.

Microsoft Defender for Endpoint Plan 2: extending endpoint security

Microsoft Defender for Endpoint Plan 2 adds advanced investigation, threat-hunting and response capabilities for devices.

Relevant risks include:

  • a discreet attack not blocked by the initial protection mechanisms;
  • an attacker maintaining persistence on a compromised computer;
  • an incident that requires an in-depth investigation;
  • the need to respond rapidly on an affected endpoint;
  • insufficient information to reconstruct the steps of an attack.

Microsoft Defender for Office 365 Plan 2: investigating email attacks

Microsoft Defender for Office 365 Plan 2 adds advanced investigation, automated response, attack simulation and threat exploration capabilities.

Relevant risks include:

  • a phishing campaign targeting several employees;
  • a malicious message already delivered to multiple mailboxes;
  • difficulty identifying recipients, clicks and related actions;
  • insufficient user preparation against phishing attempts;
  • a slow response after an incident has been detected.

Microsoft Defender for Cloud Apps: improving control of cloud services

Microsoft Defender for Cloud Apps helps IT teams identify and manage the cloud applications used by employees.

Relevant risks include:

  • transferring documents to a personal cloud service;
  • using an application that has not been approved by the organisation;
  • limited visibility over the SaaS services in use;
  • access to business data from an application whose risk has not been adequately assessed.

Signals from identities, endpoints, email and cloud applications can be brought together in Microsoft Defender XDR. This supports the investigation of attacks that affect several parts of the information system.

When the organisation needs to centralise threat detection, investigation and incident monitoring, the service:defender-sentinel-deployment can help define the scope, data sources and expected operating model.

Purview Suite: strengthening data protection and governance

The Microsoft Purview Suite for Microsoft 365 Business Premium extends the Purview capabilities already available in Business Premium.

It helps the organisation identify, classify, protect and govern sensitive data. Business Premium is also a prerequisite for this add-on.

Identifying and classifying sensitive information

Microsoft Purview can help the organisation recognise selected categories of sensitive information and apply classification rules.

Relevant risks include:

  • financial documents stored without a clear classification;
  • HR information accessible to an unnecessarily broad group;
  • confidential contracts treated as ordinary documents;
  • limited visibility over the location of sensitive data.

Applying sensitivity labels

Sensitivity labels indicate the confidentiality level of content and can be associated with protection controls.

Relevant risks include:

  • confidential documents being sent without protection;
  • sensitive files being shared with unauthorised people;
  • users being unsure which precautions apply to a document;
  • inconsistent protection across teams.

Extending data loss prevention

Data loss prevention policies identify and govern selected actions that could expose sensitive information.

Relevant risks include:

  • personal information being sent to an external address;
  • financial data being transmitted through an inappropriate channel;
  • excessive document sharing;
  • sensitive information being copied to an unauthorised destination;
  • accidental disclosure of customer or employee data.

Managing retention and the information lifecycle

Retention capabilities support the application of retention and deletion rules according to business requirements.

Relevant risks include:

  • premature deletion of information that should be retained;
  • indefinite retention of data that is no longer required;
  • difficulty finding documents during an audit or dispute;
  • inconsistent manual application of retention periods.

Improving visibility into insider risks

Insider risk capabilities can help the organisation identify selected behaviours that may create a risk to business data.

Relevant risks include:

  • unusual downloading of a large volume of documents;
  • transfer of sensitive information before an employee leaves;
  • inappropriate use of confidential content;
  • risky actions performed accidentally or deliberately by an authorised person.

These capabilities should be implemented with governance, privacy and proportionality requirements appropriate to the organisation.

Supporting investigations and audits

Microsoft Purview provides audit and investigation capabilities that improve visibility over activity within Microsoft 365.

Relevant risks include:

  • inability to determine who accessed, changed or deleted information;
  • insufficient evidence during an internal investigation;
  • difficulty responding to an audit request;
  • insufficient records after an incident involving business data.

The Microsoft Purview data protection and compliance service helps translate these capabilities into policies suited to the organisation’s information, users and obligations.

Licensing requirements should be reviewed before deployment. Users to whom a Purview service is applied, or who benefit from that service, must have the appropriate licence. A policy covering the entire organisation may therefore require all affected users to be licensed.

Security, compliance and backup address different needs

These areas are complementary, but they do not serve the same purpose:

  • cybersecurity aims to prevent, detect and respond to attacks;
  • data protection identifies sensitive content and controls how it is used;
  • compliance helps the organisation apply and demonstrate selected internal or regulatory requirements;
  • backup provides an independent copy that can be used to restore data after deletion, alteration or an incident.

Microsoft Defender and Microsoft Purview address security, data protection and governance requirements. They should not be presented as an independent backup solution.

A complete strategy must consider cyber-threat prevention, data governance and restoration separately. The MC3 Cloud backup and disaster recovery services can help assess data protection, restoration scenarios and business continuity requirements.

Building the right combination for each user profile

Business Basic and Business Standard remain suitable when their capabilities match the user’s actual requirements.

Business Premium becomes particularly relevant when the organisation needs to manage devices, control access and strengthen the protection of endpoints, email and business information.

The Defender and Purview suites then address more advanced risk levels. Their assignment should reflect a user’s exposure, access rights and the sensitivity of the data they handle rather than their job title alone.

If the organisation plans to deploy Microsoft 365 Copilot, the assessment should also cover permission quality, existing sharing practices, information classification and governance policies. Copilot should be introduced into an environment where users have access only to the information they genuinely need.

The objective is not to assign the most comprehensive licence to every employee. The right balance combines productivity, security, governance, administrative simplicity and cost control.

Start with a licence and security assessment

Before changing subscriptions or deploying Copilot, it is useful to review:

  • licences currently assigned;
  • applications actually used;
  • devices accessing business resources;
  • security controls already configured;
  • users with sensitive access rights;
  • permissions applied to SharePoint, Teams and OneDrive;
  • data requiring classification or additional protection;
  • potential overlap with third-party products;
  • separate requirements for security, compliance and backup.

A Microsoft 365 security assessment provides a starting point for reviewing the environment. It can be complemented by a service:microsoft-cost-license-optimization to align subscriptions more closely with actual requirements.

Based on the findings, MC3 Cloud can then support:

  • migration to the target environment;
  • identity and endpoint management;
  • data protection and governance with Microsoft Purview;
  • the implementation of a backup and disaster recovery strategy.

The objective is to build a coherent combination of Microsoft 365 Business Basic, Business Standard, Business Premium, Defender Suite, Purview Suite and, where appropriate, Microsoft 365 Copilot.
